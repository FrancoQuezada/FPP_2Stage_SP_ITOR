\begin{abstract}
Firebreak placement under uncertain ignition and propagation is a stochastic
network decision problem. Directed Cell2Fire scenario graphs retain alternative
fire routes but make optimization with many scenarios costly. We optimize
on reduced shortest-path graphs, predominantly directed acyclic graphs
rather than trees. Scenario recourse is characterized by reachability,
giving an optimal integral burn vector and relatively complete recourse.
We derive downstream, capped-coverage and propagation-path lower bounds:
the complete path family recovers fractional recourse on directed graphs,
and complete coverage coincides with it on rooted trees. The matched
reduced 20-by-20 panel has 810 runs under expected loss, CVaR and
mean--CVaR. At a 1,800-second limit and requested relative gap $10^{-3}$,
projected PathLLBI reaches the tolerance in 39, 21 and 30 of 45 cases
per objective, versus 26, 15 and 15 for the LP-dual root baseline.
Mean and maximum gaps and observed time means and sample standard
deviations are reported over all runs. Budget-conditioned results reveal
objective-dependent certification patterns.
A separate 1,440-run Sub20 campaign compares polynomial and separated
inequalities. Incomplete Reburn data, displaced placement/evaluation
fields and missing original scenario graphs prevent a validated
reduced/Reburn loss comparison or claim about placement quality.
\end{abstract}
