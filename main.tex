\begin{abstract}
Firebreak placement under uncertain propagation is a stochastic network
decision problem. We formulate it on reduced directed scenario graphs,
characterize reachability recourse and derive downstream, coverage and
path lower bounds. The complete path family recovers fractional recourse
on directed graphs; complete coverage agrees with it on rooted trees.
A matched 810-run study on reduced 20-by-20 instances compares six
configurations under expected loss, CVaR and mean--CVaR. At a 1,800-second
limit and relative MIP tolerance $10^{-3}$, Combinatorial Branch-and-Benders
certifies 95.6\% of expected-loss instances (43/45), with mean final gap 0.18\%.
Projected path inequalities give the strongest recorded outcomes for
CVaR (46.7\% certified; mean gap 3.51\%) and mean--CVaR (66.7\%; mean
gap 2.10\%). We report certification, final gaps and observed time
with all unresolved runs included and examine training-size and budget
dependence. A run-level audit identifies 24 CVaR and 15 mean--CVaR
sampled problems without a certified solution from any compared method.
Missing intact placements and scenario inputs preclude a supported
out-of-sample stability or paired reduced-to-Reburn fidelity estimate.
\end{abstract}
