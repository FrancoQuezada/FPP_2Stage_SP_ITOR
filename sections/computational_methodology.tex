\section{Computational methodology and provenance}
\label{sec:computational}

We distinguish the \texttt{fpp\_new20x20\_scaling} campaign from the
\texttt{fpp\_projected\_llbi\_scaling} campaign on \texttt{Sub20}. The
principal numerical panel uses only the reduced \texttt{new20x20} rows of
the first campaign. Its Reburn rows are incomplete and cannot be used as
a paired evaluation. The second campaign is reported separately because
it uses another landscape, different samples and additional methods.
For reproducibility, the counts and tables in this article can be
reconstructed by \texttt{analysis/reproduce\_results.py} from the
committed batch files in the implementation repository.

\subsection{Recorded design and graph classification}

The \texttt{new20x20} manifest declares a 400-cell landscape,
$\alpha\in\{0.01,0.02,0.03\}$, four training sample sizes
$n\in\{100,200,400,800\}$ and five replications per configuration.
It includes both \texttt{shortest\_path} and \texttt{reburn\_all\_paths}
inputs, each with three objectives and six methods per objective: 2,160
planned runs in all. For every recorded method comparison within an
instance, budget, objective and replication, the manifest specifies the
same training and test split files. Training identifiers are drawn from
1--9000 and the fixed 1,000 test identifiers are 9001--10000. The
recorded solver limit is 1,800 seconds, requested relative MIP gap
$10^{-3}$ and one thread. CVaR uses $\beta=0.9$; mean--CVaR uses
$\lambda=0.5$. These are manifest settings, not inferences from the
launcher's defaults. The committed result contains only $n=100,200,400$
for the reduced landscape; no $n=800$ solve is claimed.

The preflight file reports graph classes for 10,000 reduced
\texttt{20x20} scenario IDs: 12.41\% rooted arborescences, 86.83\%
acyclic DAGs that are not trees, and 0.76\% empty or invalid. The
corresponding Reburn ratios include 94.26\% general directed graphs.
These classifications refer to the preflight output, not to independent
reconstruction from the unavailable \texttt{new\_instances/20x20} and
\texttt{new\_instances/20x20\_reburn} arc and ignition files. In
particular, identical cell sets and ignition IDs, and the purported
reduced-to-Reburn arc inclusion, have not been independently checked.
The tree-only identity \eqref{eq:tree-equality} therefore cannot explain
the performance of the predominantly DAG reduced panel in general.

The separate \texttt{Sub20} manifest contains 1,440 runs on a Canadian
FBP landscape: the same three budgets, $n=100,200,400,800$, five
replications, three objectives and eight methods per objective. It has
200 test scenarios per run, the same 1,800-second limit, $10^{-3}$
requested gap and one thread. Its run-level graph classifications also
include many DAGs. Neither its test set nor its results are pooled with
\texttt{new20x20}. No completed \texttt{new40x40} run is in the two
campaigns under analysis.

\subsection{Compared methods and measurements}\label{sec:compared}

Table~\ref{tab:methods} lists the six configurations per objective in
the \texttt{new20x20} manifest (18 labels). \texttt{RootCuts} uses
LP-dual root user cuts in addition to lazy optimality cuts at integer
candidates. The standard LLBI is an initial structural inequality;
\texttt{exp} denotes root separation of the projected family. The
manifest records up to ten projected separation rounds and 100 cuts per
round, with a $10^{-6}$ violation tolerance. These are configured
ceilings and do not assert that each run attained them. The path
separator enumerates a limited number of paths per node (default eight
in the implementation); the manifest does not record a per-run override
of that path cap. The combinatorial method has its own cut construction
and candidate checks; its 0.10 initial sampling fraction does not allow
an unchecked candidate to be accepted. The panel has one combinatorial
ordering and has no projected \texttt{poly} variants.

The \texttt{new20x20} consolidated file has 1,329 rows but a malformed
132-column header: the last ten headings are blank, and fields from
\texttt{selected\_firebreaks} onward are displaced. The individual
worker CSV, JSON, solution files and intact full consolidated CSV named
by the manifest are absent from the committed results. Thus our tables
use only the uniquely named, verified fields \emph{before}
\texttt{selected\_firebreaks}: identifiers, split and configuration
metadata matched to the manifest, status, solver time, final gap and
pre-existing cut diagnostics. They make no claim about the displaced
placements, training or test evaluation fields, hardware or effective
solver version. The separate \texttt{Sub20} consolidated file has a
consistent 230-column header and explicit validation statuses.

A status of \texttt{Optimal} with final relative gap at most $10^{-3}$
is called \emph{resolved to the requested tolerance}, rather than a
proof of zero gap. All \texttt{Feasible} observations are treated as
time-limited for solver-time analyses; their recorded final gaps are
reported separately. Any runtime ratio compares only the same instance,
budget, training IDs, objective, solver limit and two methods that both
reached the tolerance. Runs lacking certification do not enter a ratio.
The fraction resolved by each time in Fig.~\ref{fig:new20-certified}
uses a common denominator of 45 per objective and method; time-limited
runs remain unresolved at the 1,800-second horizon.

\subsection{Paired graph and held-out evaluation}\label{sec:evaluation}

For a feasible reduced-graph placement $y^D$, the planned paired
quantity on the \emph{same} scenario IDs is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:paired-delta}
 \widehat\Delta_{\rm pair}(y^D)
   =\frac{1}{|\mathcal S_{\rm train}|}
     \sum_{s\in\mathcal S_{\rm train}}
         \bigl(Q_s^R(y^D)-Q_s^D(y^D)\bigr).
\end{equation}
Its sign is predicted by Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction} only after
the graph-pair premises have been verified. The current reduced and
Reburn training partitions differ in all 60 corresponding
$(\alpha,n,\text{case})$ combinations, despite sharing the fixed test
set. Their independently optimized objectives cannot be substituted
for \eqref{eq:paired-delta}. The displaced placement and evaluation
fields and absent scenario inputs further preclude paired and held-out
loss estimates in the present revision.
