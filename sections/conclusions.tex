\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}

We formulated stochastic firebreak placement on reduced directed
propagation graphs and characterized its scenario recourse by
reachability. The recourse LP has a componentwise minimal integral burn
vector for every binary placement and relatively complete recourse.
Downstream, capped-coverage and path inequalities provide valid master
bounds. Their complete projections satisfy an explicit hierarchy:
complete paths recover fractional recourse on general directed graphs,
while coverage coincides with paths on \emph{rooted trees}. The reported
reduced inputs are mostly non-tree DAGs, and the implemented path
separator retains a limited number of paths; theoretical tree
equalities must be used under their stated assumptions.

The reproducible \texttt{new20x20} reduced panel contains 810 matched
runs under expected loss, CVaR and mean--CVaR. For example, the
Path-exp method reaches the requested $10^{-3}$ gap in 39, 21 and 30
of 45 cases for the three objectives, respectively; the LP-dual root
baseline reaches it in 26, 15 and 15. These counts characterize solver
certification at a 1,800-second limit. Mean and maximum final gaps and observed time means and sample
standard deviations summarize all runs, including those stopped at the
time limit. Increasing the budget is associated with fewer
certifications for the decomposition methods in this panel; extensive
SAA under expected loss is an exception, improving from 12/15 to 15/15.
Budget-conditioned results and the reported time dispersion distinguish
these objective-dependent patterns without interpreting censored times
as completed solves. A separate 1,440-run
\texttt{Sub20} campaign permits the polynomial versus separated
projected-inequality comparison, but its 62 extensive-model validation
warnings prevent unrestricted solution-quality comparisons.

The value of the reduced representation for firebreak \emph{decisions}
remains open. The first campaign's Reburn records are incomplete; all
60 corresponding reduced/Reburn training splits differ; and the
committed summary lacks correctly aligned placements and evaluation
fields. The original graph inputs are also unavailable for checking
cell sets, ignitions and arc inclusion. A first priority is to restore
those inputs and run artifacts, and to evaluate each fixed reduced
placement on the \emph{same} scenario identifiers in both graphs.
Independent held-out evaluation and a matched spatial-scaling panel
would then support conclusions about decision quality and scalability
beyond the present reduced 20-by-20 experiments.
