\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}

We formulated firebreak placement under a finite sample of ignition and
propagation scenarios. The decision is optimized on rooted tree reductions
of directed Cell2Fire propagation graphs and then evaluated on the paired
original Reburn graphs. This design separates two questions that are often
conflated: how to solve the reduced stochastic program and how much loss is
introduced when the placement is assessed using the omitted propagation
routes. For the same placement and a verified arc-subset construction, tree
loss is a lower bound on Reburn loss; the tree optimum is not thereby a
Reburn optimum.

The scenario recourse LP has a reachable-set optimal solution for every
binary placement, including when some cell weights vanish. This gives
relatively complete recourse and supports exact LP-dual Branch-and-Benders
cuts. We developed the standard downstream LLBI, a cellwise capped
coverage bound and a propagation-path bound. Their projections explain
both valid root separation and the relative strengths of the complete
families. On rooted trees, complete coverage and complete path bounds
coincide with fractional recourse, while their specified static cuts
coincide with the standard LLBI. These equalities constrain how any
future runtime differences should be interpreted.

The present repository snapshot does not provide nonempty final solver
runs or paired graph evaluations. We consequently draw no empirical
conclusion about scalability, comparative speed, optimality gaps,
out-of-sample quality or the size of the Reburn/tree discrepancy.
Completing those comparisons requires the actual scenario graphs,
successful run-level results and solver provenance, plus checks of
rooted-tree structure, arc inclusion and common identifiers. Once
available, paired differences across objectives and treatment budgets
will reveal where a tractable tree approximation is accurate enough and
where retaining alternative propagation routes is important for
decisions.
