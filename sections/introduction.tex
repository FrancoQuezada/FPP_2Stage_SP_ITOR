\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Firebreaks interrupt the spread of fire through a landscape, but their
effect depends on where a future fire starts and which propagation routes
it follows. Their placement must therefore be decided before much of the
relevant information is known. Fuel-treatment planning has long used
spatial optimization, and two-stage stochastic models provide a way to
represent uncertain fire behavior explicitly
\citep{agee2000use,kabli2015stochastic}. A sampled wildfire scenario is
particularly useful when it preserves the geography of a simulated fire
rather than representing risk by independent cell scores.

Cell2Fire generates spatially explicit fire realizations that can support
planning decisions \citep{pais2021cell2fire}; network measures such as
downstream protection value have also been used to identify potentially
important treatment locations \citep{pais2021downstream}. When a
simulated realization is represented by a directed propagation graph,
several routes may lead to the same cell. Keeping these alternatives
matters when assessing a proposed firebreak: blocking one route need
not protect a cell that can be reached through another. The same graph
complexity, however, makes a stochastic optimization model with many
scenarios difficult to solve. A method that simplifies propagation
must therefore be judged both by its computational value and by the
quality of the resulting decisions under the original propagation
representation.

We investigate this trade-off by constructing, for each original
Reburn graph, a rooted shortest-path spanning tree on the same
scenario cells. The trees are the \emph{optimization} scenarios. A
firebreak placement selected on these scenarios is subsequently
evaluated on its paired Reburn graphs, using the same ignitions and
scenario identifiers. The reduction preserves an original route to
each cell but can delete alternatives. It consequently gives an
optimistic loss for any fixed placement. Paired Reburn evaluation
measures the discrepancy introduced by the reduction; assessing
placements on unused scenarios is a separate generalization test.

The reduced stochastic problem remains a mixed-integer optimization
problem: one treatment plan must work across many graph realizations
and, under CVaR, across adverse outcomes. Branch-and-Benders separates
the common treatment decisions from scenario propagation
\citep{rahmaniani2017benders}. Root relaxations can still underestimate
scenario loss while their recourse cuts are incomplete. We exploit
graph structure to supply valid lower estimates based on downstream
reachability, capped cell coverage and explicit ignition-to-cell paths.
The relationship among these estimates is particularly sharp on trees,
where every cell has one ignition path. Combinatorial Benders for
network spread provides a separate exact methodological comparator
\citep{taninmics2024benders}.

This paper makes four precise methodological contributions. It
formulates expected-loss and upper-tail stochastic firebreak placement
on reduced propagation graphs while separating paired original-graph
evaluation from independent testing. It characterizes scenario recourse
by reachability, establishing an optimal integral burn vector and
relatively complete recourse. It derives downstream, coverage and path
lower bounds, including projected families and a proof that the
complete coverage and path envelopes coincide with fractional recourse
on rooted trees. Finally, it integrates these bounds into an exact
Branch-and-Benders implementation and specifies a matched evaluation
protocol for optimization and graph-reduction quality. The repository
snapshot supplied for this revision does not contain completed numerical
runs, so the paper draws no empirical performance conclusion.
