\section{Results and discussion}\label{sec:results}

The two campaigns answer different computational questions. The first
provides a complete \emph{reduced-graph} method panel at three training
sizes; the second supplies an independent, complete \texttt{Sub20} panel
including both \texttt{poly} and \texttt{exp} variants. Neither provides
a verified placement-by-placement reduced/Reburn comparison. We report
certification and solver timing from the well-formed prefix of the
\texttt{new20x20} consolidated CSV and keep decision-quality claims
separate from these computational results.

\subsection{The reduced \texttt{new20x20} panel}

Of 2,160 tasks in the first manifest, 1,329 appear in its partial
consolidated file: all 810 reduced runs for $n=100,200,400$ and 519
Reburn runs. Each reduced training size has 270 runs, with five cases,
three budgets, three objectives and six methods; the reduced panel
contains 424 statuses \texttt{Optimal} at the requested $10^{-3}$
tolerance and 386 time-limited feasible incumbents. There are no
completed $n=800$ rows. All 519 recorded Reburn runs are marked
\texttt{Feasible}, none \texttt{Optimal}; they cannot support a matched
solver or graph-quality comparison with the complete reduced panel.

At $n=400$, the coverage and path root separators certify respectively
7 and 10 expected-loss cases, against 6 for the LP-dual root baseline;
for mean--CVaR the corresponding counts are 5, 5 and 5. Across all 45
mean--CVaR cases, Path-exp reaches tolerance in 30, Coverage-exp in
22, and the root baseline in 15. For expected loss, the extensive SAA
model and combinatorial method reach 42 and 43; for CVaR they reach 6
and 19. These are \emph{certification counts} on this finite panel,
not measured improvements in placement quality or assertions about
other landscapes. The methods compared in each block share the
instance, budget, training-ID file, risk settings and 1,800-second
limit. An \texttt{Optimal} status here means resolved to the requested
tolerance, not mathematically exact zero gap.

Table~\ref{tab:new20-gaps-times} summarizes the same 45 cases for every
method within an objective. Under expected loss, Combinatorial B\&B
resolves 43/45, with mean and maximum gaps of 0.18\% and 4.00\% and
observed time $212.4\pm427.1$\,s (mean $\pm$ sample SD). Path-exp
resolves 39/45, with gaps of 0.95\% and 10.78\% and time
$356.5\pm632.3$\,s. Extensive SAA resolves 42/45 and has a lower mean
gap of 0.75\% than Path-exp, but its maximum gap is 21.79\% and its
time is $409.8\pm545.8$\,s. Thus the mean gap alone conceals differences
in the most difficult recorded cases.

For CVaR, Path-exp resolves 21/45, with mean and maximum gaps of
3.51\% and 12.40\%, versus 15/45 and 11.18\% and 26.60\% for Root.
Their observed times are $1003.5\pm872.4$\,s and
$1218.3\pm834.2$\,s. For mean--CVaR, Path-exp resolves 30/45 with
gaps of 2.10\% and 10.86\% and time $813.1\pm824.9$\,s; Root
resolves 15/45 with gaps of 8.75\% and 22.41\% and time
$1217.2\pm836.4$\,s. These jointly reported measures support the
observed computational advantage of Path-exp over Root on these risk
objectives. The time statistics describe computation consumed under
the stopping rule, not expected time to certification: time-limited
runs remain censored, and their recorded times are retained even when
they exceed the nominal 1,800-second limit.

Separation cost helps interpret the observed behavior without
isolating a causal effect. Across 135 reduced runs of each projected
method, the median recorded projected-separation time is 9.21\,s for
Coverage-exp and 1.63\,s for Path-exp; the median projected-cut count
is 1,000 for both. The separator can reach the configured ten rounds
and 100 cuts per round. Its reported initial root-bound field is zero
for all 270 projected rows, so the available root-bound change fields
are not a comparable controlled diagnosis of which family intrinsically
strengthens the same root LP. The theoretical complete-path envelope
dominates coverage on a general graph, but Path-exp stores a limited
path set and these data also reflect construction cost, cut selection
and solver interactions. The predominantly DAG classification means the
tree equality \eqref{eq:tree-equality} is not a blanket explanation of
the coverage/path timing difference.

\subsection{Effect of the firebreak budget}\label{sec:alpha-results}

Table~\ref{tab:new20-alpha-solved} conditions certification on the
budget fraction $\alpha$. Each cell contains 15 runs: five replications
at each of $n=100,200,400$. Tables~\ref{tab:alpha-Expected}--\ref{tab:alpha-MeanCVaR}
in Appendix~\ref{app:alpha} provide the corresponding mean and maximum
gaps and observed time means and sample standard deviations. The CVaR
confidence level remains $\beta=0.9$; $\alpha$ changes the firebreak
budget. These are empirical associations within this panel, not an
isolated causal diagnosis of the solver's response to budget.

\begin{minipage}{0.98\linewidth}\centering\small
\captionof{table}{Reduced \texttt{new20x20}: $N_{\rm opt}/15$ by budget
fraction, objective and method. Each column uses the same three
training sizes and five replications.}
\label{tab:new20-alpha-solved}
\input{tables/new20_alpha_solved}
\end{minipage}
\end{center}

For expected loss, Root and Standard fall from 15/15 at $\alpha=0.01$
to 11/15 at 0.02 and 0/15 at 0.03. Root's mean gap increases from
0.04\% to 0.86\% and 12.43\%, and its maximum from 0.10\% to
4.80\% and 15.71\%. Its observed time increases from
$21.7\pm15.5$\,s to $813.0\pm728.5$\,s and $1800.9\pm0.5$\,s.
The declining certification and increasing mean gap, maximum gap and
mean time also hold within each training size. The small final time
SD reflects the common stopping limit. Extensive SAA shows a different
pattern: 12/15, 15/15 and 15/15, with mean times of
$578.0\pm705.3$\,s, $401.8\pm539.9$\,s and $249.5\pm298.8$\,s.
Its certification gain comes entirely from $n=400$ (2/5, 5/5, 5/5);
at $n=100$ and 200 all runs are certified at every budget. Moreover,
its mean time at $n=100$ is non-monotone (33.5, 31.6, 39.9\,s), so
the pooled decrease is not a universal budget trend. Its mean gap also
rises slightly from 0.02\% to 0.03\% between the two larger budgets,
while remaining within tolerance on every run.

For CVaR, certification counts are non-increasing with $\alpha$ for
every method at each training size. Path-exp changes from 15/15 to
6/15 and 0/15, with mean gaps 0.07\%, 3.07\% and 7.40\% and
maximum gaps 0.10\%, 8.23\% and 12.40\%. Its mean times and SDs
are $29.0\pm39.6$\,s, $1176.5\pm808.1$\,s and
$1804.9\pm3.0$\,s. The gap increases and mean-time increases also
hold separately at $n=100,200,400$. At the largest budget, none of
the six methods certifies a CVaR run; Path-exp and Coverage-exp still
have smaller maximum gaps (12.40\% and 15.90\%) than Root (26.60\%).
The contraction in Path-exp's time dispersion at $\alpha=0.03$
therefore accompanies universal time limitation, not faster resolution.

For mean--CVaR, Path-exp retains 15/15, 10/15 and 5/15 as the budget
increases. Its mean gaps are 0.06\%, 1.84\% and 4.41\%, its maxima
0.10\%, 7.26\% and 10.86\%, and its times
$22.8\pm21.6$\,s, $884.5\pm807.1$\,s and $1532.0\pm508.1$\,s.
The non-increasing certification counts and increasing mean gaps hold
within each training size. The five certifications at the largest
budget comprise four at $n=100$, one at $n=200$ and none at $n=400$.
At $n=400$, mean times at the two larger budgets are both near the
limit (1810.0 and 1806.3\,s), with a slight decrease despite the
higher gap. Other metrics need not follow certification monotonically:
Extensive SAA's mean gap is 22.07\%, 14.41\% and 16.80\%, and
Combinatorial B\&B's maximum gap is 14.25\%, 8.03\% and 16.29\%.
These exceptions reinforce the need to report both gap summaries and
time dispersion alongside $N_{\rm opt}/N$.

\subsection{Independent \texttt{Sub20} projected-inequality panel}

The \texttt{Sub20} consolidated file contains all 1,440 manifest runs
for four training sizes and 200 test scenarios per run. Of these, 605
have \texttt{Optimal} status at the requested tolerance. All 1,260
Branch-and-Benders rows have \texttt{validation\_status=pass}; all 62
warnings occur among the 180 extensive FPP-SAA rows: 50 CVaR, seven
mean--CVaR and five expected-loss runs. Nine warned CVaR runs are even
marked \texttt{Optimal}. The validation code compares the solver's
expected-loss component with a reachability-based evaluation of the
selected placement. A warning therefore signals disagreement of those
two values; it must not be described as a direct test that the CVaR
value equals expected loss. We exclude extensive-model solution-quality
comparisons pending investigation of these discrepancies.

For the expected-loss \texttt{Sub20} cases, both projected \texttt{exp}
methods certify 37 of 60, compared with 32 for Coverage-poly and 33
for Path-poly. For CVaR and mean--CVaR, the gap in counts is smaller.
Coverage-poly is mathematically the standard all-unsaturated cut when
defined on identical scenario and candidate sets; these finite-run
counts alone cannot establish a different complete bound. The
\texttt{Sub20} run-level classifications also contain many non-tree
DAGs, so tree-only identities cannot explain all its runtime
differences. Different landscapes, sample counts and test sets prevent
cross-campaign performance ratios.

\subsection{Placement evaluation and Reburn discrepancy remain open}

In the first campaign, 14 reduced and 57 Reburn FPP-SAA rows have
\texttt{objective\_in\_sample}>400, all of them time-limited CVaR or
mean--CVaR incumbents. An extensive-model incumbent may contain burn
variables above the componentwise minimal reachable vector for a fixed
placement; minimizing them after fixing the placement could decrease
its objective. Without the corresponding selected cells, original
scenario records and correctly aligned evaluation fields, that
mechanism cannot be verified for these rows. A recorded extensive
objective is consequently not the evaluated fire loss of its
placement, and no reduction-benefit percentage is inferred from it.

The reduced/Reburn training-ID lists disagree in all 60 matching
budget, sample-size and replication pairs. The first campaign's
Reburn panel has only 519 of the planned 1,080 rows and no certified
solve. Moreover, the committed file lacks the scenario arc/ignition
inputs and intact worker solutions needed to map one reduced placement
onto the corresponding Reburn IDs. Thus \eqref{eq:paired-delta}, its
CVaR analogue and held-out losses cannot currently be estimated.
The \texttt{profiles\_newInstancas\_20x20/joined\_profile\_*} images
are excluded because their denominators and graph mixture cannot be
recovered from a generating script. Figure~\ref{fig:new20-certified}
is instead regenerated from the complete, single-graph reduced panel
with an explicitly fixed denominator and censoring rule.
