\section{Results and discussion}\label{sec:results}

The distinction between mathematical predictions and measured performance
is especially important here. The source snapshot contains the experiment
scripts and solver implementation, but its result and manifest files are
empty. We therefore first give a fully specified analytical check of the
tree/Reburn distinction and then state the empirical comparisons that
cannot yet be evaluated. No times, gaps or solution-quality gains are
imputed from the scripts or from the superseded results section.

\subsection{A constructed check of the reduction and bounds}

Consider one unit-weight scenario with ignition $r$, eligible candidates
$C=\{a,b\}$, and original arcs
$E^R=\{(r,a),(r,b),(a,k),(b,k)\}$. A rooted shortest-path
tree can choose $E^T=\{(r,a),(r,b),(a,k)\}$. For the placement selecting
only $a$, the remaining tree burns $\{r,b\}$, whereas the original graph
also burns $k$ through $b$. Thus $Q^T(y)=2$ and $Q^R(y)=3$.
Table~\ref{tab:constructed} gives the resulting lower bounds, computed
directly from the definitions in Section~\ref{sec:methods}.

On the original graph, treating $a$ can potentially cover $k$ but does
not block the $r$--$b$--$k$ route. Complete PathLLBI accounts for that
route and reaches the actual loss. On the tree the two complete envelopes
coincide, as predicted by \eqref{eq:tree-equality}; the missing arc
explains the one-cell paired loss increase. This example checks the
direction and possible strictness of \eqref{eq:graph-monotonicity}, but
it says nothing about the frequency or magnitude of such discrepancies
in the intended landscapes.

\subsection{Scalability and recourse strengthening}

The first empirical question is whether graph inequalities help solve
larger sampled tree models. The appropriate comparison is among
methods matched on tree instance, training IDs, risk objective, weight
map, treatment budget, tolerance and limit. For each group, a results
table should show the number of certified solves and time-limited
runs, paired runtime among jointly solved instances, and final gap for
unsolved runs. Root bounds, cut counts and separation time can then
help distinguish bound changes from the time spent obtaining them.

There is an important theoretical constraint on interpreting that
comparison. On verified rooted trees, complete CoverageLLBI and
complete PathLLBI define the same fractional recourse bound. A different
runtime between their implemented root separators would therefore
reflect finite cut generation or computational behavior; it would not
establish an ordering of their complete tree envelopes. Likewise, the
static projected coverage and static one-path-per-cell cuts coincide
with standard LLBI on a tree. The available files do not reveal whether
any configuration reduced runtime, improved root bounds or solved more
instances, so these outcomes remain open.

\subsection{Paired Reburn loss and independent scenarios}

The second empirical question is the price paid for deleting
alternative propagation arcs. A valid paired analysis evaluates each
tree-selected placement on its exact training IDs in both graphs,
checks that selected original cell IDs and weights match, and reports
\eqref{eq:paired-delta} alongside the paired expected and upper-tail
losses. Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction} predicts the sign of each
paired difference when arc inclusion is satisfied, but it places no
useful upper limit on their aggregate size. A difference of zero would
show only that the omitted routes do not change the loss for that
placement and those scenarios.

Independent test IDs answer a different question: how a placement
trained on some realizations performs on unseen \emph{tree} scenarios.
For each matched run, test mean and empirical CVaR can be compared with
the corresponding training metrics. A strong in-sample objective or a
small paired graph discrepancy does not alone establish generalization.
The repository snapshot contains neither successful paired evaluations
nor held-out losses, so no claim about transfer quality or risk
performance can presently be made.

\paragraph{Empirical evidence status.}
The supplied implementation checkout records 6,390 files in
\texttt{Results\_server}, of which none contains data; its 3,384 CSV files are
also zero bytes. The \texttt{new\_instances} scenario inputs and a CPLEX-enabled
executable are not included. Thus this manuscript gives a validated
structural analysis, a verified description of the configured study and
a constructed check, but \emph{not} a completed numerical assessment.
The matched scalability and graph-reduction tables and figures must be
generated from nonempty, provenance-checked final campaign files before
empirical conclusions or a submission-ready abstract can be added.
